The first project I did without a written agreement went fine. The second one did not, and the issue was not bad faith; it was two people remembering a call differently. Since then every engagement has a short agreement, and the version below is what it has become. Have a lawyer check yours; what follows is what has proven useful, in plain language, with the reason each clause is there.

## 1. Scope, by reference

"The work is described in the scope document dated X, attached as Schedule A. Anything not in Schedule A is out of scope."

The agreement itself does not describe the software. The discovery document does, and it is attached. When the question "was this included?" comes up, both sides open the same document. This clause and the one on changes are what prevent the slow scope growth from the estimation post.

## 2. Changes

"Requests outside Schedule A will be estimated separately and begin only after written approval of the estimate. Minor adjustments under [threshold] are included at my discretion."

Written means an email is enough. The threshold keeps small reasonable things from needing paperwork.

## 3. Payment milestones and late fees

"Fees are payable as follows: [percentage] on signing; [percentage] on acceptance of each milestone in Schedule B; the balance on handover. Invoices are due within 15 days. Overdue amounts carry interest at [rate] per month."

Milestones are tied to deliverables the client accepts, not to dates. The late-fee clause has been invoked exactly once and resolved the invoice within a day; its value is in existing.

"Acceptance" has its own line: a milestone is accepted when the client confirms in writing, or ten working days after delivery if no issues are raised. Without this, acceptance can be deferred forever.

## 4. Intellectual property

"On receipt of full payment, all rights in the deliverables transfer to the client. Until then, the deliverables are licensed to the client for evaluation. I retain the right to reuse general techniques, libraries and non-client-specific code, and to describe the project in my portfolio subject to clause 8."

Transfer on payment, not on delivery, is the clause that protects the final invoice. The reuse line means the open-source helpers and patterns written for one client are not locked away. The portfolio line is negotiated per client; most agree to an unnamed description at minimum.

## 5. Accounts and access

"Hosting, domain, database, email and third-party service accounts are created in the client's name, paid by the client, and I am granted access for the duration of the engagement. On handover, my access is removed and the client holds all credentials."

The client owns their infrastructure from day one. I have inherited projects where the previous developer owned the domain, and it is a miserable situation for everyone. This clause avoids creating it.

## 6. Confidentiality

Mutual, covering business information and data, surviving the agreement. Standard and short. Personal data handled under the client's policies, and deleted from my machines at handover.

## 7. Warranty and support

"For 30 days after handover, defects in the deliverables as described in Schedule A are fixed at no charge. Support beyond that, and changes, are under a separate maintenance arrangement."

A defined warranty period ends the "is this a bug or a change" argument by giving bugs a window and changes a price. The maintenance arrangement is a monthly retainer with a fixed number of hours, in its own one-page addendum.

## 8. Portfolio and references

Whether the project can be named, whether screenshots can be used, and whether the client will be a reference. Three checkboxes. Agreed before the project, not asked for after, when it is awkward.

## 9. Termination

"Either party may end the engagement with 15 days' written notice. The client pays for work completed to the date of termination, and receives the deliverables completed and paid for."

Projects end early for reasons that have nothing to do with the work: a business changes direction, a budget disappears. A clean exit clause makes that a conversation instead of a dispute.

## 10. Liability and the boring parts

Liability capped at the fees paid. No consequential damages. Governing law and jurisdiction: the courts where I am, which is Surat. Disputes to mediation first. These have never been invoked, and the lawyer who reviewed the agreement said they are the ones that matter if anything ever goes seriously wrong.

## What never came up

Non-compete clauses, which I decline. Clauses about working hours or tools, which do not belong in a contract for deliverables. Penalty clauses for delay, which I accept only alongside a clause making the client responsible for delays they cause, at which point most clients drop both.

## The practical bits

The agreement is four pages. It goes as a PDF, is signed electronically, and is referred to in the first invoice. GST registration and invoicing requirements in India are a separate topic; a chartered accountant's hour is well spent there.
